DFlash speculative decoding on AWS Trainium2

Aayush Poudel · NYU × Annapurna Labs “Hack the Chip” hackathon · October 2026

Code and reproduction Raw results Follow-up work DFlash model Paper vLLM-Neuron

DFlash is a block-diffusion draft model for speculative decoding: a small model proposes several future tokens in one pass, and the Qwen3-8B target verifies those proposals together. vLLM supported the model on GPU, but the installed Neuron backend failed while loading it (DFlashQwen3ForCausalLM had no from_configs method). This work added the Neuron draft model, proposer, Qwen3 hidden-state export, weight loading, and runner dispatch.

Measured result at k=4: bidirectional query-block attention raised draft acceptance from 20.1% to 32.7% and increased Qwen3-8B decode throughput from 1.34x to 1.70x over the autoregressive baseline.

Benchmark

This is a small hackathon benchmark: one request at a time, greedy decoding, three prompts repeated twice, and 128 generated tokens per request. Throughput is total completion tokens divided by total request wall time across those six requests. k is the number of draft positions proposed per target step. Draft acceptance is accepted draft tokens divided by all proposed draft tokens.

Every row used one Trainium2 chip with tensor parallelism over two NeuronCores (TP=2), a 256-token model length, and the same vLLM settings. The sample is too small to characterize production performance; the per-request median and range show the observed spread.

Effect of the query-block mask

The DFlash draft was trained with bidirectional attention inside its proposed-token block. The first Neuron version reused the causal decode mask. Replacing that active-block mask with an all-ones mask improved k=4 acceptance from 20.1% to 32.7% and throughput from 69.3 to 88.1 tok/s.

At k=8, the largest absolute increases in accepted tokens were at positions 0 and 1 (+34 and +36); the largest relative increases were at positions 1 and 2 (+44% and +31%). Positions 4–6 were unchanged or slightly lower. Bidirectional access to the proposed-token block is a likely explanation, consistent with the training configuration, but these counts alone do not establish causality.

Reproducibility and scope

By default the installer applies the bidirectional mask used for the headline result. bash apply.sh --causal reproduces the earlier causal build. Each committed result log records its mask mode and patch state. The exact commands, tested container image, raw logs, implementation notes, and known limitations are in the project README.

The served checkpoint is DFlash (z-lab/Qwen3-8B-DFlash-b16), not the later DFlash 2 architecture. A separate set of component checks for the later Qwen3.8-27B DFlash 2 model is documented as follow-up work; it is not an end-to-end server.

The speculative k=4 output matched the baseline's greedy text for roughly 95 tokens and then diverged on a near-tie, consistent with bf16 differences between single-token and multi-token target kernels. Accepted drafts are checked by the target, but no bit-exact output claim is made.
